# Launch and hackathon presentation
## Verified deadline and rules
Deadline shown by FirstCommit: 30 September 2026 at 5:00 pm EDT, equivalent to 21:00 UTC and 1 October at 2:00 am Pakistan time. Aim to submit on 29 September Pakistan time, retaining the final day as buffer.

Official rules require work during the event, a public repository with meaningful development history (single-commit upload disqualifies), attribution and significant AI disclosure in README. All teammates must contribute meaningfully and explain their work. AI may support learning but must not replace understanding or the majority of the work. Up to six teammates are permitted. These requirements matter when implementing this generated specification. [Official rules](https://firstcommit.devpost.com/rules).

## Four-minute demo plan — proposed
0:00–0:25: Show the learning problem and Errby's teacher/learner inversion.
0:25–0:55: Teacher enters a sparse topic list, reviews a generated lesson, publishes and shows the class code.
0:55–1:15: Student joins/selects lesson; show the genuine opening question.
1:15–2:10: Student explains, Errby asks a mistaken follow-up, student agrees wrongly, Supervisor intervenes visibly.
2:10–2:45: Student corrects with a new example; objectives update from real persisted evidence.
2:45–3:15: Student summary and teacher roster with scoped one-word status and actual metrics.
3:15–3:45: Team explains source preparation, evaluator, privacy boundaries and the hardest bug fixed.
3:45–4:00: State tested scope, genuine learning, limitations and next step.

Use clearly labelled synthetic users and a reviewed short lesson. For expensive/waiting preparation, show a real recorded segment honestly labelled; do not fake instantaneous generation. A live path must still exist. Do not manufacture school partnerships, testimonials or learning improvements.

## Contribution narrative
Lead: learned/implemented state, evaluation, persistence and security.
Beginner: explain one accessible component, its props, an interaction bug and how it was tested.
Third teammate: show content-validation sheet, actual user observation or QA report and how it changed the product.
Each person's evidence is tangible. No invented commit history or retroactive claims.

## Submission checklist
Repository public and free of secrets/data; README setup commands tested on clean machine; environment variable names without values; migration/seed instructions; technologies and licences; AI assistance description including this pack and generated mockups; known limitations; working URL and judge instructions; 3–5 minute video based on supplied event requirements; screenshots with synthetic labels; exact team contributions.
Provide a fallback recorded demonstration if hosted AI temporarily fails, clearly distinguished from live functionality.

## Release gate
All P0 requirements and critical tests pass; no unverified goal counted complete; school route works; budget control exercised; teacher cannot see another class; supervisor role readable; no real child records in judge accounts. Day-13 feature freeze. Fix critical defects only thereafter.

## School demonstration gate
A teacher walkthrough with synthetic/adult responses can validate usability first. Actual pupils participate only after school approval and provider/data conditions in PRIVACY_AND_SAFETY.md are met. If no pupil trial occurs, say so.
