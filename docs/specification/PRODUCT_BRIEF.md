# Product brief
## Problem and intended outcome
Students may recognise a definition without being able to explain why it is true. Errby gives them an audience to teach, targeted misunderstandings to correct, and feedback on the concepts they can explain. This is the intended benefit, not a demonstrated efficacy claim.

Primary-school through middle-school pupils and high-school students are the intended audience. English only. The context is schools with expected individual access to phones, laptops or Chromebooks, plus independent sessions based on the learner's own topic or material. A specific school is a possible pilot, not a committed partner.

## Goal hierarchy
1. A coherent, reliable, explainable FirstCommit submission in about 14 days.
2. A complete learning interaction with trustworthy visible feedback.
3. A functional teacher workflow that needs little preparation.
4. A small, honestly reported school/teacher usability exercise if access and appropriate safeguards are ready.
No revenue, subscriptions or long-term institutional rollout is required for this hackathon.

## Core promise
Bring a topic, resource or school lesson. Explain it to Errby. Correct its misunderstanding. Leave with evidence of what you explained correctly and what needs another attempt.

## Entry routes
- Independent: describe a topic, paste material or provide a resource. Errby asks only missing scope questions and prepares a lesson.
- School: teacher creates a class, uploads a syllabus/topic list or learning material, reviews generated lessons and shares a join code.
A short outline must be sufficient to begin lesson drafting; the school need not supply complete teaching notes.

## Required differentiators
The learner does most of the explaining. Errby's misconceptions are intentional and limited to the lesson. A distinct supervisor intervenes after submitted student answers when needed. Progress depends on concept evidence rather than chat length. Teacher analytics show a one-word, topic-specific status with inspectable reasons.

## Success measures for the prototype
An unfamiliar tester can create/join a lesson and submit a first explanation without coaching. A prepared evaluation suite checks whether correct explanations are accepted and false agreement triggers intervention. Every completion has valid evidence for every required objective. A teacher can create a class and publish reviewed lessons from a short topic list. The final demo shows this loop without manual database edits.

Record counts, sample size, observed failures and context. A few tests can reveal usability problems; they cannot establish educational effectiveness.

## Non-goals
Native mobile/desktop apps, voice, video calls, full video-frame analysis, school enterprise SSO, billing, public social feeds, competitive ranks, automated high-stakes grading, every subject/curriculum fully validated, and large-scale textbook ingestion.
